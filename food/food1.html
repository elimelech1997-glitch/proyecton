Shawarma

The origin of shawarma is from Turkish cuisine, where it is called "Döner Kebab" (meaning "turning meat") and in Greek "Gyros" (meaning "turn"). The name shawarma also originates from the Turkish language, and is the Arabic form of the word çevirme (meaning "to turn or turn"). The word "shawarma" in Turkey is currently used only for a whole animal skewered and grilled over a fire. Shawarma/döner is sold in every country in the Middle East and in all cities around the world where there are immigrant communities from Middle Eastern countries. Over the years, the term "shawarma" has also come to be used as a name for a form of cutting raw meat (usually beef or lamb) in which the meat is cut into thin, narrow strips before cooking, and the cook who cooks it can grill it on a frying pan or in an oven dish. This method produces grilled meat pieces that resemble the shawarma sold in stalls and restaurants. Thus, in parallel with traditional shawarma, "home shawarma" was created, which does not require specialized equipment to prepare it.

  • Marinate the Meat In a large bowl, whisk together the olive oil, garlic, lemon juice, and all the spices. Add the thinly sliced meat, tossing well to ensure every piece is evenly coated. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1–2 hours (overnight yields the best flavor)
  • Build the Stack (Oven Method) Preheat your oven to 200°C (400°F). Cut a large onion in half to use as a sturdy base and place it cut-side down on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Insert a long wooden skewer vertically into the center of the onion. Thread the marinated meat slices onto the skewer one by one, pressing them down firmly to create a tight stack. Top with the other onion half or a tomato half
  • Roast to Perfection Roast vertically in the oven for about 40–50 minutes, until the edges are crispy and charred while the inside remains juicy.
  • Slice and Serve Let the meat rest for 5 minutes. Using a sharp knife, thinly carve the meat downwards from the spit. Serve warm inside a fresh pita or laffa with tahini, Israeli salad, and pickles. Tip: For a quick stovetop alternative, skip the skewer setup and sear the marinated meat slices in a blazing-hot cast-iron skillet in small batches for 3–4 minutes per side until crisp
