  • The origins of falafel are not known for certain. The accepted hypothesis is that it originated in the province of Egypt
    • (Aegyptus, before the Arab conquest), around the first half of the first millennium AD, where Egyptian ful was prepared. Falafel was probably developed by the Copts as an alternative dish to meat on holidays when eating meat was forbidden.[1] The dish later spread north to the Levant, where it was mainly prepared with chickpeas.[2] Today, falafel is common throughout the Middle East, including Israel, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, and Yemen. Following the immigration of Arabs and Israelis to the United States and Western Europe, it can also be found in major cities there since the mid-20th century. Traditionally, falafel is deep-fried and served with tahini and salad, as a pita sandwich, or as an appetizer. In the early 21st century, some people bake falafel, for health reasons and to maintain a healthy body weight. Due to the widespread distribution of falafel over such a long time, many versions of the dish have been created. However, two main types can be distinguished: small balls of coarsely ground chickpeas, or medium-sized patties made from fava beans, which are mainly common in Egypt and Sudan and are called ta'ama there. In Egypt, beans are also used to prepare the dish. In the Land of Israel, the dish was common among the Arab residents and in the 1930s it also began to be popular among the Jewish community.[3][4] In the first decades of the state's existence, when falafel was a staple fast food, falafel was perceived as a national dish, even though it originated in Egypt. This can be seen in the "Falafel Song" by the Ayalon Band from the 1950s. In a public opinion poll conducted in 2022, the majority of respondents also chose falafel as the national dish.[5] Other references to the dish in Israeli culture include the popular online game "Falafel King" (2002), the film "Atomic Falafel" (2015), and the IDF slang term for officers of major and above. Israelis adopted the Lebanese version of falafel made from chickpeas. With the arrival of immigrants from Arab countries, falafel became more popular and relatively inexpensive. Some argue that the real reason for the end of the custom of making falafel from chickpeas was the fact that some Iraqi immigrants are sensitive to chickpeas, and therefore the complete switch to chickpeas was made.[6] Many falafel stands in Israel serve a pita dish with hummus, fries, salad, and tahini, but the hummus and fries have been absent from the older stands.     Falafel is a traditional Middle Eastern dish deep-fried in oil in the form of a ball or patty made from chickpeas, beans, or a combination of both. Falafel balls are served in pita or lafa, and are often accompanied by salads, pickled vegetables, sahug, amba, hummus, and tahini. Falafel dishes are very common as street food throughout the Middle East. And in countries where immigrants from these countries live.
